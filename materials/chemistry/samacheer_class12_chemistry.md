# Unit 1: Metallurgy (உலோகவியல்)

<!-- page: 1 -->

Metallurgy refers to the science and technology of extracting metals in their pure form from their natural ores.
A mineral is a naturally occurring substance obtained by mining which contains the metal in free state or in combined form.
An ore (தாது) is a mineral from which a metal can be extracted profitably, easily, and economically. All ores are minerals, but not all minerals are ores (e.g., Bauxite is an ore of aluminium, whereas clay is merely a mineral).

Methods of Concentration of Ores:
1. Gravity Separation or Hydraulic Wash:
Used when there is a significant difference in specific gravity between ore particles and gangue (e.g., tin stone SnO2 and hematite Fe2O3).
2. Froth Flotation Process (நுரை மிதப்பு முறை):
Used for sulfide ores such as Galena (PbS), Zinc blende (ZnS), and Copper pyrites (CuFeS2).
The powdered ore is mixed with water and a frothing agent (pine oil or eucalyptus oil) along with a collector (sodium ethyl xanthate). Gangue particles are wetted by water, while ore particles are wetted by oil and float with the froth.
3. Magnetic Separation:
Used when either the ore or the gangue is ferromagnetic (e.g., separating magnetic Wolframite FeWO4 from non-magnetic Tinstone SnO2).
4. Leaching (வேதி முறையில் பிரித்தல்):
Dissolving the metal into a soluble complex using a suitable chemical reagent such as Cyanide leaching for Gold (MacArthur-Forrest process), Ammonia leaching, or Alkali leaching (Bayer's process for bauxite Al2O3.2H2O using hot concentrated NaOH).

<!-- page: 2 -->

Refining of Crude Metals:
1. Zone Refining (மண்டல தூய்மையாக்கல்):
Principle: Fractional crystallization. When an impure molten metal is allowed to solidify, impurities remain preferentially in the molten zone because they are more soluble in the liquid melt than in the solid state.
Used for ultrapure semiconductor elements: Germanium (Ge), Silicon (Si), and Gallium (Ga).
2. Mond's Process for Refining Nickel:
Nickel is heated with carbon monoxide at 350 K to form volatile nickel tetracarbonyl Ni(CO)4:
Ni + 4 CO -> Ni(CO)4 (350 K)
The volatile complex is then decomposed by heating to 450-470 K to yield pure nickel:
Ni(CO)4 -> Ni + 4 CO (460 K)
3. Van Arkel Method for Zirconium and Titanium:
Used for obtaining ultrapure Zirconium (Zr) and Titanium (Ti).
Impure metal is heated in an evacuated vessel with iodine to form volatile tetraiodide:
Ti + 2 I2 -> TiI4 (550 K)
The vapour is then passed over a red-hot tungsten filament at 1675 K where it decomposes into pure metal:
TiI4 -> Ti + 2 I2 (1675 K)


# Unit 2: Solid State (திட நிலைமை)

<!-- page: 3 -->

Classification of Solids:
Solids are classified into crystalline solids and amorphous solids.
Crystalline solids have long-range order, sharp melting points, definite heat of fusion, and are anisotropic (physical properties vary along different crystal directions).
Amorphous solids lack regular order, melt over a range of temperatures, and are isotropic.

Types of Cubic Unit Cells:
1. Simple Cubic (SC): Atoms present only at corners.
Total number of atoms per unit cell = 8 * (1/8) = 1 atom.
Coordination number = 6. Packing efficiency = 52.4%.
2. Body-Centered Cubic (BCC): Atoms at corners plus one in center of cube.
Total atoms = 8 * (1/8) + 1 = 2 atoms.
Coordination number = 8. Packing efficiency = 68%.
3. Face-Centered Cubic (FCC): Atoms at corners and at each face center.
Total atoms = 8 * (1/8) + 6 * (1/2) = 4 atoms.
Coordination number = 12. Packing efficiency = 74%.

Bragg's Law of X-Ray Diffraction:
2 * d * sin(theta) = n * lambda
where d is interplanar distance, theta is diffraction angle, n is diffraction order, and lambda is X-ray wavelength.

Crystal Defects (படிகக் குறைபாடுகள்):
1. Schottky Defect (ஷாட்கி குறைபாடு):
Stoichiometric defect occurring when an equal number of cations and anions are missing from the crystal lattice.
Results in a decrease in the density of the crystal without altering chemical stoichiometry.
Observed in ionic solids with high coordination numbers and similar ionic radii: NaCl, KCl, CsCl, AgBr.
2. Frenkel Defect (பிரங்கல் குறைபாடு):
Occurs when a smaller ion (usually cation) is displaced from its regular lattice site to an interstitial position.
The crystal density remains unchanged.
Observed in ionic solids with large radius differences between cations and anions: ZnS, AgCl, AgBr, AgI.
Notice: Silver bromide (AgBr) exhibits both Schottky and Frenkel defects.


# Unit 3: Solutions and Electrochemistry (கரைசல்கள் மற்றும் மின்வேதியியல்)

<!-- page: 4 -->

Raoult's Law (ராவுல்ட் விதி):
For a solution of volatile liquids, the partial vapour pressure of each volatile component is equal to the product of the vapour pressure of the pure component and its mole fraction in solution:
P_A = P_A^0 * x_A
An ideal solution obeys Raoult's law over the entire range of concentration, has Delta_H_mix = 0, and Delta_V_mix = 0 (e.g., Benzene + Toluene, n-Hexane + n-Heptane).

Colligative Properties (துகள் எண்ணிக்கை பண்புகள்):
Properties of dilute solutions that depend only on the number of solute particles, not on their nature:
1. Relative lowering of vapour pressure: (P_0 - P) / P_0 = x_solute
2. Elevation of boiling point: Delta_T_b = K_b * m (where K_b is molal boiling point elevation constant or ebullioscopic constant).
3. Depression of freezing point: Delta_T_f = K_f * m (where K_f is molal freezing point depression constant or cryoscopic constant).
4. Osmotic pressure (சவ்வூடுபரவல் அழுத்தம்): pi = C * R * T = (n / V) * R * T
Van 't Hoff Factor (i):
i = Observed colligative property / Calculated colligative property
For association, i < 1; for dissociation, i > 1 (e.g., for NaCl, i = 2; for BaCl2, i = 3).

Electrochemistry Fundamentals:
Nernst Equation:
Relates cell EMF to standard EMF and reaction quotient Q:
E_cell = E^0_cell - (2.303 * R * T / (n * F)) * log10(Q)
At 298 K (25 degrees Celsius):
E_cell = E^0_cell - (0.0591 / n) * log10(Q)
where n is the number of electrons transferred in the redox reaction and F is Faraday's constant (96,485 C mol^-1).

Kohlrausch's Law of Independent Migration of Ions (கோல்ராஷ் விதி):
At infinite dilution, when dissociation of electrolyte is complete, each ion makes a definite and independent contribution towards the molar conductivity of the electrolyte, irrespective of the nature of the other ion with which it is associated:
Lambda_m^0 = nu_+ * lambda_+^0 + nu_- * lambda_-^0
Applications: Determination of limiting molar conductivity of weak electrolytes like acetic acid (CH3COOH):
Lambda_m^0(CH3COOH) = Lambda_m^0(CH3COONa) + Lambda_m^0(HCl) - Lambda_m^0(NaCl).

Faraday's Laws of Electrolysis:
First Law: Mass m of a substance deposited or liberated at an electrode is directly proportional to the quantity of electricity Q passed:
m = Z * Q = Z * I * t (where Z is electrochemical equivalent).
Second Law: When the same quantity of electricity is passed through different electrolytes, the masses of substances deposited are proportional to their chemical equivalent weights:
m1 / m2 = E1 / E2.


# Unit 4: Chemical Kinetics (வேதி வினைவேகவியல்)

<!-- page: 6 -->

Rate of Reaction and Rate Law:
Rate of reaction is the change in concentration of a reactant or product per unit time:
Rate = - d[Reactant] / dt = + d[Product] / dt
For a reaction aA + bB -> Products, Rate Law is:
Rate = k * [A]^x * [B]^y
The overall order of reaction is x + y.
Molecularity is the number of reacting species colliding simultaneously in an elementary reaction. Molecularity is always a positive integer and cannot be zero or fractional, whereas order can be zero, fractional, or negative.

Zero-Order and First-Order Integrated Rate Equations:
1. Zero-Order Reaction: Rate = k * [A]^0 = k.
Integrated equation: [A] = [A_0] - k * t.
Half-life period: t_1/2 = [A_0] / (2 * k).
2. First-Order Reaction: Rate = k * [A].
Integrated equation: k = (2.303 / t) * log10([A_0] / [A]).
Half-life period: t_1/2 = 0.693 / k.
Notice that the half-life of a first-order reaction is completely independent of the initial concentration of reactant [A_0].

Arrhenius Equation and Activation Energy:
k = A * exp(- E_a / (R * T))
where A is frequency factor, E_a is activation energy (கிளர்வுறு ஆற்றல்), R is gas constant (8.314 J K^-1 mol^-1), and T is absolute temperature.
Taking natural logarithms:
ln(k2 / k1) = (E_a / R) * (1 / T1 - 1 / T2)


# Unit 5: Coordination Compounds (அணைவுச் சேர்மங்கள்)

<!-- page: 7 -->

Werner's Coordination Theory (வெர்னர் கொள்கை):
Alfred Werner (Father of Coordination Chemistry) proposed that metals in coordination compounds exhibit two types of valency:
1. Primary Valency (முதன்மை இணைதிறன்): Ionizable, non-directional, corresponds to the oxidation state of the central metal ion, satisfied by negative ions.
2. Secondary Valency (இரண்டாம் நிலை இணைதிறன்): Non-ionizable, directional, corresponds to the coordination number (அணைவு எண்) of the metal, satisfied by neutral molecules or negative ions. Secondary valencies dictate the stereochemistry and geometrical geometry (octahedral, tetrahedral, square planar) of the coordination complex.

Crystal Field Theory (CFT - படிக புலக் கொள்கை):
Considers the bond between central metal ion and ligands as purely electrostatic.
In a free transition metal ion, all five d-orbitals are degenerate (same energy).
When ligands approach the central metal ion in an Octahedral crystal field:
The degenerate d-orbitals split into two sets:
1. Lower energy t2g set (d_xy, d_yz, d_zx) with energy -0.4 * Delta_o.
2. Higher energy eg set (d_x2-y2, d_z2) with energy +0.6 * Delta_o.
The energy separation between the two sets is called Crystal Field Splitting Energy Delta_o.
For strong field ligands (CN-, CO, en), Delta_o > P (pairing energy), giving low-spin complexes.
For weak field ligands (I-, Br-, Cl-, F-), Delta_o < P, giving high-spin complexes.


# Unit 6: Organic Chemistry - Carbonyls and Amines (கார்போனைல் சேர்மங்கள்)

<!-- page: 8 -->

Important Named Organic Reactions:
1. Aldol Condensation (ஆல்டால் குறுக்கம்):
Aldehydes or ketones possessing at least one alpha-hydrogen atom undergo self-condensation in the presence of dilute base (NaOH or Ba(OH)2) to form beta-hydroxy aldehydes (aldols) or beta-hydroxy ketones. On heating, they eliminate water to yield alpha,beta-unsaturated carbonyl compounds (e.g., Acetaldehyde gives Crotonaldehyde CH3-CH=CH-CHO).

2. Cannizzaro Reaction (கானிசரோ வினை):
Aldehydes with no alpha-hydrogen atoms (Formaldehyde HCHO, Benzaldehyde C6H5CHO) undergo disproportionation (self oxidation-reduction) when treated with concentrated alkali (50% NaOH):
One molecule is reduced to corresponding alcohol, while the other is oxidized to carboxylic acid salt:
2 C6H5CHO + NaOH -> C6H5CH2OH (Benzyl alcohol) + C6H5COONa (Sodium benzoate).

3. Clemmensen Reduction:
Carbonyl group (>C=O) of aldehydes and ketones is reduced directly to methylene group (>CH2) using Zinc amalgam and concentrated hydrochloric acid (Zn-Hg / conc. HCl).

4. Wolff-Kishner Reduction:
Carbonyl compounds treated with hydrazine (NH2-NH2) followed by heating with potassium hydroxide (KOH) in ethylene glycol are reduced to hydrocarbons.

5. Carbylamine Test (ஐசோசயனைடு ஆய்வு):
Aliphatic and aromatic primary amines (1 degree amines) when warmed with chloroform (CHCl3) and alcoholic KOH form offensive smelling isocyanides (carbylamines):
R-NH2 + CHCl3 + 3 KOH -> R-NC + 3 KCl + 3 H2O
Secondary and tertiary amines do not show this reaction, making it an excellent diagnostic test for primary amines.
